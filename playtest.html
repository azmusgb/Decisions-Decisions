BRING THE GROUP YOU WOULD ACTUALLY PLAY WITH.

Family, friends, teens, party-game regulars, and casual players are all useful. The game has to work outside the designer's living room.

What happens: play the current physical or browser prototype, finish a game, then answer a few short questions about clarity, choices, funny moments, slow spots, and replay interest.

Privacy: the browser prototype does not require camera or microphone access. Game state stays on the device unless you explicitly export data or submit feedback.

Your information is used to organize playtesting and respond to your request. See the privacy page.

WHAT YOU DO

LEARN IT. PLAY IT. REACT.

The prototype should do the explaining. If something needs rescuing, that is useful feedback.

1LEARN

Use the provided instructions and see whether the game makes sense quickly.

2PLAY

Finish a full game without strategy coaching.

3NOTICE

Pay attention to choices, laughs, confusion, and slow moments.

4REACT

Tell us what you would keep, change, or play again.

WHAT MATTERS MOST

DID THE CHOICE MAKE THE GAME BETTER?

We are testing the experience, not asking people to be polite about a prototype.

UNDERSTANDING

Did it click quickly?

Could the group understand why the three point values matter without repeated explanation?

CHOICE

Did the points change picks?

Did players choose differently from prompt to prompt instead of defaulting to one comfortable option?

REPLAY

Did you want another round?

Laughter matters, but genuine replay interest is the stronger signal.

THE GOAL

THEY GET IT. THEY LAUGH. THEY WANT ANOTHER ONE.

That is the milestone we care about before making bigger product decisions.
