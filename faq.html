FAQ

QUESTIONS, ANSWERED.

Current answers reflect the active prototype and product truth. Anything still unresolved is labeled accordingly instead of being presented as final.

What is GET THE POINT?

A social party game built around choice under time pressure. Each prompt offers multiple communication routes with different prompt-specific point values. You choose one, commit, perform, and your teammates try to guess before time runs out.

Is this just Pictionary, charades, and humming in one box?

No. The differentiator is the decision before the performance: one prompt, multiple routes, different risk/reward, and the player chooses which route to take.

What are the current routes?

The v2.1 challenger is HUM → DRAW → MIME using teal, yellow, and purple. These labels, order, colors, and the MIME icon are still PROPOSED / NEEDS PLAYTESTING.

Why HUM instead of SOUND?

HUM is the current challenger because it creates a clearer musical constraint. A broader SOUND route remains an active test condition.

How does scoring work?

Every prompt has its own point values by route. The harder-looking option is not permanently worth more; scoring is prompt-specific and should eventually be calibrated with observed playtest data.

What is the timer?

The current baseline is 60 seconds, then 30, then 15, plus a 15-second tiebreak condition. These are test defaults, not final commercial rules.

Can players pass?

Pass/skip behavior is unresolved. The working demo exposes pass/no-pass as a playtest variable instead of hard-coding a permanent answer.

Does the timer start when the prompt appears?

This is still being tested. The digital prototype can compare timer-start-on-reveal versus timer-start-on-commit behavior.

Is GET THE POINT the final commercial name?

No. It is the current lead/name-to-beat. PICK YOUR POINT is the serious challenger, YOUR CALL is a wildcard, and POINT TAKEN is a control. Consumer preference and legal/trademark clearance are separate workstreams.

What will the physical game include?

Current prototype direction is roughly 100 double-sided cards, a reusable drawing surface, dry-erase marker, quick-start rules, simple scoring, and a phone timer. Final materials, dimensions, dielines, and finishes require supplier validation.

Is there an app?

There is a working offline-capable PWA demo now, and the preferred product direction is iPhone-first, offline, pass-and-play. The MVP avoids accounts, social features, voice recognition, camera/video, subscriptions, and unnecessary permissions.

Will there be subscriptions?

Not by default. The preferred model is a free or low-friction starter, a one-time Core unlock, optional one-time theme packs, and a separate physical edition.

Will there be licensed packs?

Possibly later, but launch economics will not depend on licensed Disney/NFL/NBA/Marvel-style content. Rights safety is a content gate.

What is the current success milestone?

Strangers understand the game, laugh, make meaningful choices, and want to play again. The team should not spend heavily on manufacturing or a full commercial app before blind-play evidence validates the mechanic.
